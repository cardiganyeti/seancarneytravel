Five Questions to Answer Before You Book a Big Trip

By Sean Carney · November 2026

Most trips start with a place. Somebody mentions Portugal, or a ship, or a friend's photos from Kyoto, and the planning begins with the map. But in my experience the place is rarely what makes or breaks a trip. The questions underneath it are.

I was a leadership coach for years before I became a travel advisor, and I still start every trip the way I used to start a coaching conversation: with a few questions before any answers. These are the five I ask myself, and the five I'd ask you.

1. How do you want to feel when you get home?

Not what you want to see. How you want to feel walking back through your own front door. Rested? Inspired? Closer to the person you went with? Proud that you finally did the thing you kept putting off?

Pick one. It sounds soft, but it's the most practical question on the list, because it settles a dozen decisions later. If the answer is "rested," a nine-city itinerary is out before anyone opens a map. If it's "proud," maybe the hard trek stays in.

For my last crossing, the answer was "reset." That one word is why I booked eighteen nights on Queen Mary 2, most of them at sea, in my own slightly bougie little stateroom. I read, I wrote, I thought, and mostly I watched the water go by out the window. It turns out the sea is meditative without you having to try very hard.

2. What wore you out last time?

Everybody has a version. The 5 a.m. tour departures. The long-haul flight with no break. Four hotels in eight days. A group that wanted to do everything together. I'm an introvert, so for me it's usually too much company before coffee.

Whatever it was, plan it out of this trip first, before you add anything fun. It's much easier to protect your energy at the planning stage than to find it again halfway through the trip.

3. How many times do you want to pack?

Every move between hotels eats into a day: the checkout, the transfer, the new neighborhood, finding the nearest decent coffee all over again. Two or three places with real time in each will almost always show you more than seven places seen from a suitcase.

This is one reason I love ships, and especially crossings. You unpack once, and the scenery does the moving.

4. Where should the money go?

Every budget has a few places where spending makes the whole trip better, and plenty where it doesn't. For me, it's the stopover hotel that breaks up a long flight, the night before a cruise, and a location I can walk from. I'll happily take a window cabin instead of a balcony on a cold, rainy itinerary and spend the difference on something I'll remember.

Your list will be different. The point is to decide on purpose, so the money goes to what you'll remember or what keeps you going, and not to what you'll barely notice.

5. What would help you arrive well?

This is the one people skip, and the one that matters more every year. What does the body you're bringing need to enjoy this trip? A night in the city before the ship sails, so a canceled flight doesn't cost you the cruise. A stopover on the way to Europe. A docked port instead of a tender. An itinerary with a sea day in the middle.

If a cane, a walker, a scooter or a knee with opinions is part of the picture, this is where it belongs: in the plan from the start, not a problem to solve at the gangway. The right cabin, the right ports and the right pace are what let you keep saying yes to the trips you want. There's more in tender ports explained and planning a cruise with a parent who uses a walker or wheelchair.

Then pick the place

Once you've answered these, choosing the destination gets easier, because half the options rule themselves out. What's left is a short list of trips that actually fit you.
