How Much Does a Travel Agent Cost for a Cruise or a Big Trip?

By Sean Carney · November 2026

For the trips I book, you don't pay me a planning fee. The cruise lines, hotels and other suppliers pay me a commission when you book. That's one way advisors work, but not the only one, so here's how it all works and what to ask.

How travel advisors get paid

There are three common setups:

  • Commission. The supplier pays the advisor after you book. You pay the supplier for the trip, the same as you would on your own, and there may be no separate fee.
  • Fees. You pay the advisor for research, planning or designing an itinerary, sometimes whether or not they book it.
  • Both. A planning fee plus commission, or fees for some services and not others.

A fee isn't a red flag. You're paying for someone's time and know-how, and fees usually depend on how complicated the trip is. What matters is knowing the arrangement before you start.

What "no planning fee" means with me

I work on commission. You pay the cruise line or hotel for the trip, plus any taxes and extras, and they pay me. Nothing extra comes from you for the planning.

My business depends on people traveling with me again and telling their friends. So a trip that actually suits you is worth far more to me than one that pays a little more.

What you get besides the booking

A cruise or a big trip is more than picking a ship or a hotel. There's the cabin and where it sits on the ship, getting to the pier, hotels on either side, which ports use tenders, and a schedule with enough room in it to enjoy.

It starts with a conversation by phone, video or text: what you want from the trip, who's going, your budget, and what would make it comfortable. Then I research and bring you a plan to react to. You make the decisions. I handle the bookings and the details we agree on, and I'm reachable while you're away if something needs sorting. I can't control every airline or prevent every delay, but you'll have a real person to call.

Are perks included?

Sometimes. Depending on the hotel and the booking, I may be able to add things like breakfast, a hotel credit, or an upgrade if one's available. They're nice when they happen, but they're not guaranteed, and I'll tell you exactly what's confirmed. The room, the location and the terms matter more than a maybe-upgrade.

When an advisor is worth it, and when to book it yourself

An advisor earns their keep when a trip has a lot of moving parts: a cruise with hotel nights and transfers, a long journey with several stops, a family trip with different budgets and wishes, or a trip where the details of the cabin, the shower or the walk to dinner matter to someone in the group.

If you already know exactly what you want and it's a simple booking, doing it yourself may be quicker. And if the only goal is the lowest possible price, a booking site is built for that. I'm most useful when you want the trip to fit.

What to ask any advisor

  • How are you paid for this trip?
  • Is there a planning fee? If so, what does it cover, when is it due, and is it refundable?
  • Could change or cancellation fees apply later?
  • What support do I get before and during the trip?
  • Which rates or perks are confirmed, and which depend on availability?
