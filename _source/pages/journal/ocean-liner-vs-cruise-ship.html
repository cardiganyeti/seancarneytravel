Ocean Liner vs. Cruise Ship: What a Crossing Gives You That a Cruise Can't

By Sean Carney · November 2026

People ask me why I'd spend a week getting to Europe when a plane takes a night. The honest answer is that the week is the point.

A liner and a cruise ship aren't the same thing

An ocean liner is built to cross an ocean: from one place to another, on a schedule, in whatever the North Atlantic is doing that week. A cruise ship is built to be a vacation at sea, with the ports as the main event. Queen Mary 2 is the only ocean liner in regular service, and on a crossing she does exactly what she was built for.

What a crossing day is like

There are no ports, no excursions to book and no tender lines. The day has a shape anyway: breakfast, a few laps of the promenade deck, a talk or a film, lunch, a nap I'm not ashamed of, and the light going orange on the water.

The clocks change by an hour on most nights, so the time difference happens a little at a time instead of all at once in an airport. You unpack once, and the cabin is yours for the week.

Who a crossing suits

People who'd rather arrive rested than arrive fast. People who find airports harder every year. Anyone heading to or from Europe for a long stay, since you can bring far more luggage than an airline allows. And anyone who has always wanted to do it once.

Accessibility on board

Getting around with a wheelchair or scooter

Queen Mary 2 has wheelchair-accessible staterooms, and elevators connect the public decks. On a ship this size, the bigger question is distance. Some cabins are a long walk from the elevators and the dining rooms, so that's the first thing I check.

Hearing, vision and other needs

Before booking, I ask the line the specific questions that matter to you, whether that's an orientation walk when you board, visual alerts in the cabin, or arrangements for a service animal, and you get the answers before you commit.

Ports at both ends

On the classic New York to Southampton crossing, the ship docks at both ends, so there are no tender boats to manage.

Rough days at sea

The North Atlantic is the North Atlantic. Queen Mary 2 handles it well, but some days the deck moves. A cabin lower down and toward the middle of the ship moves the least.

Would I book this for a client?

Yes, for anyone who wants the journey to count as part of the trip. I'd book a night in New York or Southampton before sailing, so nothing about embarkation day is rushed, and leave the day you arrive open.
