If the Queen Mary 2 Refit Has You Upset

By Sean Carney · November 2026

I like change, until it's mine.

Cunard is taking Queen Mary 2 into dry dock at Damen Shiprepair Brest, in France, from March 31 to May 9, 2027. She'll come out with 31 new staterooms, more outdoor deck space, redesigned Grill suites, and refreshed kennels and spa. If you've sailed her more than once, you probably have a corner you think of as yours, and you might be wondering whether it'll still be there.

I get it. I'm excited about change in the world and a lot less excited about change in me, and I'm turning 60 in April, so I've had plenty of practice noticing the difference.

It's fine to feel it

When a place has been part of how you travel for years, a change to it can feel personal, especially when someone else made the decision. Nobody needs to talk you out of that.

Why ships get refits

Upkeep is how a ship keeps sailing. A liner that crosses the North Atlantic year after year needs work to stay comfortable and worth booking, and new cabins help keep her on that route. The refit isn't the end of the ship you love. It's how she keeps going.

She was the new one once

When Queen Mary 2 took over the transatlantic run from the QE2 in 2004, she was the unfamiliar newcomer. A lot of what regulars love about her now was new then.

Before or after?

If you want one more crossing with her as she is now, there's still time to sail before she goes in. If you're curious what's new, sail after. Both are good reasons to go.
