It starts with you, not a brochure

Most trip planning starts with a destination. I start with you: what you want from the trip, what you'd like to come home with, and how you like to spend a day when nobody's in charge of it. I was a coach for years, and it turns out the questions that help people sort out their lives work pretty well on vacations too.

Then we plan it together. I don't know every ship and hotel by heart, but I know what to ask, and I stand behind the plan. When something changes halfway through, you have someone in your corner who already knows it. There's no planning fee. Here's how I'm paid.

Who I plan with

You've just stepped back

A big career or a big title is behind you, and you want a trip that feels like a reset, not a checklist. The month in one place, the ocean crossing, the route through four countries by ship and train. If it has a lot of moving parts, we keep track of them together.

You travel on your own, by choice

A good ship, a walkable city, a quiet season and your own pace. Solo doesn't have to mean making do. I have a soft spot for cooler places, like the Scottish islands or the Netherlands in autumn, but Italy in October works on the same thinking.

You're bringing everyone together

The big birthday, the reunion, the trip with the kids and grandkids, planned so the oldest and the youngest both have a good time, and you come home rested instead of needing a vacation from your vacation.

You want to keep going

Luxury, river and small-ship voyages planned around how you travel now, so you can keep traveling well for as long as you want. Whatever you bring along is part of the plan from the first conversation.

Planned around you

The best trip is the one that fits how you travel now, so you can keep doing it for a long time. My own best days involve a window, a pot of tea and nowhere to be until lunch. Yours might be a new port every morning. Both are good trips.

Whatever you bring along is part of the plan from the first conversation: a knee that doesn't love stairs, a wheelchair, a hearing aid, low vision, a need for a quiet afternoon. On every trip, I check things like:

  • How far your room is from the elevator and the dining room
  • Whether the shower has a step
  • Whether a port is docked or reached by tender boat
  • How much walking a day really involves
