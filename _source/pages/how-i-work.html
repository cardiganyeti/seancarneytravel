How I work

No planning fee, a lot of questions, and someone to call when the plan changes.

From your first note to coming home

  1. You tell me about your trip

    Fill in the short form or send me an email. A rough idea is plenty. I reply within 24 hours.

  2. We talk

    Thirty minutes to an hour, by phone, video or text, whichever you prefer. I'll ask about dates and budget, and also about what you want from the trip and how you'd like to feel when you get home.

  3. I put together a plan

    Big decisions come first, because they shape everything after: where, then which ship or hotel, then the cabin and the extras. Some trips take one conversation. Bigger ones take a few.

  4. You decide, and I handle the rest

    You make the decisions. I make them easier, then take care of the bookings, the transfers and the special requests, including letting the hotel know it's your anniversary.

  5. I'm around the whole way

    Before you go, while you're traveling, and after you're back. I'll want to hear how it went.

What I check, for everyone

Some of the most important parts of a trip aren't in the brochure. For every trip I plan, I look at:

  • Where your cabin or room sits: how far from the elevator, the dining room and the quiet spaces.
  • The bathroom. A step-in shower and a roll-in shower are very different things, and photos don't always tell you which one you've got.
  • Ports: whether the ship docks or you go ashore by tender boat, which changes the whole day.
  • How much walking a day really involves, and where you can sit down.
  • Airport and port assistance, booked ahead instead of requested on the day.
  • Transfers in a vehicle that fits you, your luggage and anything you're bringing, like a scooter.
  • Time to rest, built in on purpose.

If you travel with a service animal, use a hearing aid, have low vision, or need anything else, I'll ask the line or the hotel the specific questions before you book, not after.

How I'm paid

I don't charge a planning fee. Hotels, cruise lines and tour companies pay me a commission when you book through me, so my help doesn't come with a separate bill.

Through Fora, some hotels add perks for my clients, like breakfast, a credit or an upgrade when one's available. They're nice. I won't promise you a red carpet.

So who do I work for? You. My business runs on people coming back and sending their friends, and that only happens if the trip was right for you.
